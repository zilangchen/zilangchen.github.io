Highlights

  • Developed a camera-free, zero-shot deformation reconstruction framework for soft robots using flexible piezoresistive sensing and cage-based 3D Gaussian modeling.
  • Designed and produced a flexible 10×10 piezoresistive sensor array (3×3 cm) that conforms to soft robot surfaces.
  • Implemented a GAT-based cage deformer with inverse-distance-weighted propagation to dense Gaussian primitives.
  • Achieved average full-body results of 0.67 IoU, 0.65 SSIM, and 3.48 mm Chamfer distance on unseen soft robots.
  • Demonstrated zero-shot camera-free reconstruction without robot-specific retraining. Paper accepted to IEEE/RSJ IROS 2026.

Project Overview

During the ISURE Summer Research Program at University of Notre Dame (Jul 2025 – Sep 2025), I worked with Prof. Tingyu Cheng to develop a unified camera-free deformation sensing and rendering system for soft robotics. This project addresses the critical need for accurate 3D reconstruction in dark, confined, or privacy-sensitive environments where traditional camera-based approaches fail.

Technical Approach

Sensor Design & Manufacturing

  • Flexible Sensor Array: Designed and produced a 10×10 piezoresistive sensor array (3×3 cm) that provides sparse pressure-induced resistance readings on the object's surface in real time. The array conforms to soft robot surfaces.
  • Sensor Integration: Attached the flexible patch to a selected surface region, where it captures tactile signals during bending and twisting.

Canonical 3D Gaussian Representation

  • Static Geometry Proxy: Initialized each undeformed object from a nominal geometric proxy, such as its fabrication STL file.
  • Gaussian Representation: Converted the canonical geometry into dense Gaussian primitives for real-time deformation and photorealistic rendering.
  • Zero-shot Deployment: Reused the trained tactile-to-cage mapping on unseen soft robots without collecting robot-specific deformation data or fine-tuning the model.

Cage-based Deformation Framework

  • Graph Attention Network: Predicted low-dimensional displacements of sparse cage-control nodes from encoded tactile measurements.
  • Inverse-Distance-Weighted Propagation: Propagated cage motion to dense Gaussian primitives while preserving global geometric continuity.
  • Boundary-aware Control: Coupled the sensed region to neighboring geometry to reduce discontinuities outside the sensor footprint.

Performance Results

  • Full-body Reconstruction: Averaged 0.67 IoU, 0.65 SSIM, and 3.48 mm Chamfer distance across unseen bending and twisting soft robots.
  • Angular Accuracy: Averaged 4.7° error for bending and 4.9° for twisting.
  • Runtime: Reached approximately 30 FPS with the coarse Gaussian setting and 5 FPS at high resolution on a single RTX 3070 Ti.
  • Additional Demonstration: Reconstructed pressure-induced deformation on a wallet as a qualitative proof of concept.

Key Innovations

  1. Sparse-to-Dense Mapping: Mapped local tactile observations to object-wide deformation through a compact, structured cage representation.
  2. Efficient Architecture: The cage-based strategy decouples low-dimensional control from high-resolution representation, enabling efficient inference and scalability.
  3. Unified Pipeline: Created a complete end-to-end pipeline from sensor design to real-time rendering, demonstrating practical applicability for soft robotics.

Applications

This camera-free deformation reconstruction system can serve as an effective solution for soft robotics and other applications in:

  • Dark or low-light environments where cameras are ineffective
  • Confined spaces with limited camera access (e.g., endoscopic scenes, minimally invasive instruments)
  • Privacy-sensitive environments where visual recording is restricted
  • Real-time manipulation tasks requiring precise geometry recovery
