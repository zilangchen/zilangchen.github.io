Project Overview

From December 2022 through July 2023, the South China University of Technology team developed two robots for the “Casting Flowers over Angkor Wat” event of the 22nd National College Student Robot Contest ROBOCON. The national finals were held in Jiangyin from July 5–8, 2023, and the team received National Third Prize.

The project followed the ABU ROBOCON 2023 game theme. A Rabbit Robot and an Elephant Robot worked together to collect and toss team-colored rings onto scoring poles arranged across an Angkor Wat-inspired field. The team had to combine reliable ring handling, maneuverability, and coordination between the two machines within each match.

Key Outcomes

  • Built and integrated a two-robot competition system for ring collection and ring tossing.
  • Completed the national competition with both robots operating on the official field.
  • Received National Third Prize in the ROBOCON 2023 “Casting Flowers over Angkor Wat” event.

Competition Challenge

Under the official rules, each team could build one Rabbit Robot and one Elephant Robot. Together, the robots collected red or blue rings and tossed them toward 11 poles. The Rabbit Robot could enter the Angkor areas and center area, while the Elephant Robot operated within its team's main zone. A team achieved “Chey-Yo” by placing its color on top of all eight designated poles.

This rule set made ring acquisition, repeatable handling, and accurate placement central to the mechanical design. The photographs below document the SCUT robots on the competition field and a ring positioned on one of the scoring poles.

Dual-Robot System

The completed system used two mechanically distinct mobile robots. Both platforms combined aluminium-profile structures, omnidirectional drivetrains, onboard electronics, and task-specific ring-handling mechanisms. Their different proportions and mechanisms reflected the separate movement zones and scoring roles defined for the Rabbit Robot and Elephant Robot.

Mechanical Fabrication and Assembly

The robots were developed through repeated fabrication, assembly, and integration work. Laser-cut parts and 3D-printed components were used alongside the aluminium-profile frames and purchased drivetrain hardware. The Rabbit Robot included a ring pickup mechanism that transferred rings into the robot's handling path.

Mechanical integration required the ring-handling components, mobile base, frame, and electronics to operate as one competition system. The project record shows the compact and tall robot configurations at different stages of workshop assembly.
