Overview

Developed a climbing-assistance hand-exoskeleton research prototype that uses pressure-based contact-state monitoring to coordinate active finger actuation and passive ratchet locking. The system was designed to preserve free hand motion during normal climbing movements and engage the lock when the measured hand-contact state did not permit a safe release.

  • Fourteen RP-L-110 thin-film pressure sensors provide hand-contact signals to a 100 Hz control loop.
  • An Arduino Uno and dedicated UART servo driver control an HX08L serial bus servo.
  • A spatial four-bar linkage provides approximately 65 mm of fingertip travel and a 2.5× mechanical advantage.
  • The complete wearable system weighed under 1.5 kg excluding the battery, including a 448.7 g hand-mounted module.

My Contribution

Led project coordination and team integration, developed the Arduino control software, supported the construction of the pressure-sensing glove, and participated in prototype fabrication, assembly, and experimental testing.

System Design

  • Sensing and control. Pressure signals from both hands are evaluated at 100 Hz. The controller permits a hand to enter transparent mode when the measured contact state supports a safe release; otherwise, it maintains the active hand in its locked state.
  • Actuation and locking. The HX08L servo drives a spatial four-bar linkage that follows the finger's extension-to-flexion motion. A cable-operated, bidirectional ratchet provides passive mechanical locking and is designed to retain the locked pose during power loss.
  • Electronics and power. An Arduino Uno runs the control logic and communicates with the serial servo through a dedicated UART driver. A single 7.4 V, 2200 mAh two-cell battery pack supplies the servo and the regulated control electronics.
  • Mechanical integration. PLA-printed linkage components are reinforced with aluminium ratchet wheels, brass standoffs, and metal joint hardware. The hand-mounted module weighed 448.7 g, and the complete wearable system excluding the battery remained below 1.5 kg.

Evaluation

  • Lock time. The mechanism reached the fully latched state in 0.18 s in 60 fps footage.
  • Repeated actuation. The fingers completed at least 90% of the full extension-to-flexion range across 50 cycles.
  • Static holding test. The grasp showed no measurable slip while holding a 16 N load on a 25 mm-diameter bar for 10 s.
  • Inclined-rig trials. Two-minute trials on a 75° climbing rig produced more than 90% successful grasp transitions. The passive ratchet remained engaged during a deliberate single-hand release test.

Prototype Scope and Limitations

This research prototype was evaluated on a controlled climbing rig. The tests characterize its locking response, repeated actuation, static holding behavior, and grasp transitions under the reported laboratory conditions; the device has not been certified as personal protective equipment.

Future Directions

Future development will investigate higher-frequency embedded control, richer contact and joint-state sensing, lighter structural materials, and more extensive endurance and safety evaluation.
